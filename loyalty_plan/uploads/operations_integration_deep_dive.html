Operations & integration deep dive

Every hard question answered. Questions first, then full answers.

Real operational problems — each one answered in depth

Q
How does a customer "check in" without a POS? How do you know they actually visited?
Critical ▾
The problem
This is the single biggest operational question for a QR-based loyalty system with no POS integration. Without linking to billing, how do you prevent a customer from sitting outside and scanning the QR to rack up fake visits without ever buying anything?
Answer — 4 options, ranked by reliability
Option 1 (MVP1 — table QR only, honour-based): QR is placed on the table. Customer must physically be sitting in the restaurant to scan it. This is good-enough for MVP because gaming requires effort: someone would need to physically visit just to scan without buying. Real-world evidence from similar platforms shows gaming rates under 3% at this stage — not worth solving technically yet. Accept it, monitor it.

Option 2 (better — time-locked QR): QR refreshes every 30–60 minutes. The code on the physical QR sticker changes. Customer must scan the current code to register a visit. This means: even if someone screenshots the QR, the code is stale within the hour. Implementation: QR display on a small digital screen at the counter or table, refreshed via the restaurant's Wi-Fi. Slightly more complex to deploy.

Option 3 (Phase 2 — staff-verify check-in): Staff confirm the visit at counter at billing time — one tap in counter mode. Pairs the loyalty check-in with an actual transaction. Requires staff discipline, not POS integration.

Option 4 (Phase 2 — bill amount entry): Customer enters their bill amount after scanning. Not auto-verified but adds friction to gaming. Also enables spend-based loop logic later.

MVP recommendation: Option 1 now, Option 2 or 3 by month 3
Q
What happens when the restaurant has no Wi-Fi or the internet is down?
Critical ▾
The problem
India's internet reliability is uneven. A restaurant in a Koramangala basement or a crowded HSR Layout space may have spotty Wi-Fi. Your system is web-based — if the customer's phone can't load the loyalty page, or if the admin counter mode can't connect, the experience breaks in the worst possible moment: when a customer is standing at the counter trying to redeem a reward.
Answer
Customer side: Phone QR scan loads a PWA (progressive web app). Once loaded once, the customer dashboard caches on their device — they can see their progress and rewards offline. The check-in action requires connectivity but is silent-queued: it fires when connectivity resumes. Most customers have mobile data even if restaurant Wi-Fi fails.

Staff/counter side: Counter mode must have offline capability for redemption. The coupon code is generated server-side and stored on the customer's device. Staff can verify the code visually (read the number, check against a recently-synced list) without real-time server hit. Add: "Last synced X minutes ago" indicator so staff know if they're offline.

Design rule: Never make redemption require a live server call. Coupon codes must be validatable offline against a local cache that syncs every 5 minutes when online. The system fails gracefully — it never blocks a redemption because of connectivity.
Q
What happens when a customer tries to redeem a reward and the staff member has no idea what it is?
High risk ▾
The problem
This kills more loyalty programs than any technical bug. A customer shows their phone at the counter on a Friday night. The staff member (who just joined 2 weeks ago) has no idea what the loyalty program is, gets confused, calls the manager, the queue builds, the customer is embarrassed, and they never use the loyalty program again. Staff turnover in Indian restaurants is extremely high — 40–60% annual churn in many Bangalore restaurants. You cannot assume training sticks.
Answer
The counter mode UI must be designed for zero training. Here's what that means concretely:

1. The coupon screen customer shows has the reward written in plain language in large text: "FREE COLD BREW — show to staff". Not a code. Not a QR. Words.
2. Staff counter mode has one job: type a 6-digit code, hit confirm. Green = valid. Red = already used/expired. No interpretation needed.
3. A laminated A5 card at every counter: "Customer showing a reward? Ask for their 6-digit code. Enter it here: [URL]." Simple enough for a Day 1 employee.
4. Reward description is repeated on the admin confirmation screen — staff can see what the reward actually is ("1 free cold brew, no minimum order") to prevent disputes.

Platform rule: design for the newest, least trained, most stressed staff member possible
Q
How do you prevent coupon fraud — sharing codes, screenshots, reusing expired coupons?
High risk ▾
The problem
Once a coupon code exists, someone will try to share it with friends, screenshot it, or use it after expiry. Indian consumers are particularly creative about this. At scale with 50+ restaurants and thousands of customers, even a 1% fraud rate becomes operationally significant.
Answer — layered fraud prevention
Layer 1 (MVP — UUID codes): Coupon codes are UUID-based (e.g. 8K4-F7P-2X1). 26^6 combinations = 300M+ codes. Not guessable. Not scannable from a photo taken 3 feet away.

Layer 2 (single-use): Every coupon can only be redeemed once. Server marks it used on first confirmation. Staff counter mode shows "already redeemed" on second attempt with timestamp and location.

Layer 3 (phone-bound): Coupon is tied to the phone number that earned it. If someone shares the code, the system flags: "This code was generated for a different phone number." Soft warning initially — hard block after 3 mismatches.

Layer 4 (time-window): Coupon is only valid during restaurant operating hours. No redemption at 3am.

Expiry handling: Once expired, code returns a clear "expired" message — not a generic error. Restaurant admin can manually reinstate a code once (for genuine cases like a customer who was sick during the validity window).
Q
How do you handle a customer who changes their phone number?
Common ▾
The problem
In India, phone numbers change fairly often — especially pre-paid SIM users. A loyal customer with 4/5 visits changes number and loses all their progress. They're angry. The restaurant has no way to verify. This is a real retention event.
Answer
Build a "phone number transfer" flow in admin. Process:
1. Customer tells staff their old number and new number.
2. Staff opens customer management, searches old number, sees their history.
3. One-click "Transfer to new number" — sends OTP to new number to verify ownership.
4. All progress, rewards, history migrates.

Also: in WhatsApp communications, begin collecting a secondary identifier (name + birthday) that can be used to verify identity without the old phone number. Not required at sign-up — collected gradually via WhatsApp conversation after 2nd visit.
Q
What if a restaurant turns off or stops using the platform mid-contract? What happens to their customers?
Business risk ▾
The problem
A restaurant closes, changes ownership, or cancels. Customers have active rewards and progress. If the system just goes dark, those customers are confused and potentially angry — and they'll blame the restaurant publicly on Zomato reviews.
Answer — offboarding protocol
When a restaurant cancels:
1. 30-day notice required in contract (6-month minimum helps here).
2. During notice period: no new sign-ups, existing customers notified via WhatsApp: "This restaurant's loyalty program is closing on [date]. Redeem your rewards before then."
3. Platform marks restaurant as "winding down" — displays message on customer page.
4. After closure: customer data exported and handed to restaurant owner (their data, not yours to keep).
5. Restaurant gets full data export: CSV of all customers, visit history, redemptions.

Never delete customer data immediately — 90-day retention after offboarding for dispute resolution

Every integration question — asked and answered honestly

Q
Should you integrate with Petpooja / UrbanPiper in MVP1?
Most asked ▾
The honest answer
No. Not in MVP1. Here's why this seems like the right move but isn't yet:

Petpooja has 80+ integrations listed — but that doesn't mean their API is clean or reliable. UrbanPiper integration is not direct, and there are a lot of issues when either Zomato or Swiggy update or modify their APIs. The same API fragility applies to any third-party integration with their POS. You'd be building on someone else's unstable foundation during your most critical phase.

Second: Petpooja already has its own loyalty module. Any restaurant on Petpooja's POS who asks "does this work with Petpooja?" and you say yes — they'll wonder why they're not just using Petpooja Loyalty. Your answer: "You can — but it doesn't do X, Y, Z that we do." Don't make integration the sales pitch. Make the experience the sales pitch.

Third: In MVP1, your visit tracking is QR-based and manual. POS integration would require building a completely different data pipeline — bill amount, SKU-level purchase tracking, real-time sync. That's a 2–3 month engineering task that delays your actual launch by the same time.
Decision
MVP1: Zero POS integration. QR-based visits only. Staff manually confirm redemptions via counter mode.
Phase 2 (month 5–8): Research Petpooja's partner API. Build a lightweight webhook — "bill closed → loyalty visit logged." This is a 4–6 week engineering task once your core product is stable.
Phase 3: Full UrbanPiper integration for multi-channel order tracking.

Rule: prove the product works without integration before you add integration complexity
Q
A restaurant already uses Reelo. How does your platform co-exist or replace it?
Sales blocker ▾
The reality
Many of your target Bangalore restaurants will already be on Reelo. This isn't a rare edge case — it's your most common prospect situation. You have three possible stances:
Three options
Option A — Replace: Full migration. You take over everything Reelo does plus more. Risk: you need to migrate their existing customer database. Reelo will not make this easy (no export API by default). You'll need to ask the restaurant to export their customer list manually (CSV) and import it into your system. Doable but requires restaurant cooperation and your team's help.

Option B — Complement: Run alongside Reelo for a pilot period. Your platform handles the gamified journey experience; Reelo keeps doing their WhatsApp blasts. This sounds compromise-friendly but creates confusion: customers get messages from two systems, staff don't know which redemption goes where. Avoid this.

Option C — Pilot with a new cohort: Don't touch their Reelo setup. Propose: "Let us run our platform for new customers who sign up from next week. After 60 days, you compare engagement. Then decide." Zero risk for them. Lets you prove the product head-to-head.

Recommend Option C for Reelo accounts. Option A for restaurants with no loyalty system yet.
Q
How does WhatsApp API integration actually work, and what are the real problems?
Technical ▾
The real picture
WhatsApp Business API (WABA) is not plug-and-play. It runs through Meta's official API and requires going through an approved Business Solution Provider (BSP). The problems most people hit:
The 6 real WhatsApp problems and how to solve each
Problem 1 — Template approval takes time: Every automated message template must be pre-approved by Meta. Approval takes 1–7 days per template. You need ~7 templates for MVP1 (welcome, nudges, unlock, expiry, win-back, birthday). Submit all templates before you start building. Don't wait until launch day.

Problem 2 — Template rejections: Meta rejects templates with promotional language ("special offer", "discount", "free") unless framed as utility messages. Your "reward unlocked" message needs careful copywriting to pass. Have 2 backup versions of every template.

Problem 3 — Number policy: Each restaurant needs their own WhatsApp Business number registered with WABA — or you operate a shared platform number. Shared number means messages say "from [platform name]" not "from [restaurant]". Restaurant-specific numbers look more authentic but require each restaurant to go through WABA registration (2–5 days). Recommendation: MVP1 uses a platform shared number with restaurant name in the message. Phase 2: restaurant-specific numbers for premium tier.

Problem 4 — Costs: Meta charges per 24-hour conversation window. India pricing: ~₹0.35–₹0.60 per utility conversation. Your 7 automated triggers = roughly 7 conversations per active customer per month. At 500 customers per restaurant: ₹1,200–₹2,100/month in raw API cost. This must be in your unit economics — it's real money.

Problem 5 — Opt-out handling: If a customer replies "STOP" to a WhatsApp message, you must immediately stop messaging them or you face Meta account suspension. Build opt-out handling on Day 1, not as an afterthought.

Problem 6 — Delivery reports: WABA gives delivered and read receipts but not always. Don't build analytics that depend on read receipts being reliable — they're not.

Best India BSPs for your use case: Interakt, Wati, AiSensy Avoid: direct Meta API if you're pre-Series A — BSPs handle compliance headaches
Q
What happens when Zomato or Instagram changes their API and your activity verification breaks?
Fragility risk ▾
The problem
You plan to let customers submit Instagram story links for verification. Instagram changes their API, or tightens link sharing, or changes story URL format. Your verification flow breaks. Restaurants start complaining activities aren't working. Customers feel cheated. This exact problem killed several third-party integrations with Zomato and Swiggy when they changed their APIs.
Answer — build for fragility from the start
Rule 1: Never auto-verify using third-party APIs in MVP1. All activity verification is manual (screenshot review by restaurant admin). This is slower but completely resilient to external API changes. No dependency to break.

Rule 2 (Phase 2): When you do automate, build an abstraction layer. Your verification logic should call an internal service ("VerificationService.verify(type, data)") that wraps the external API. When Instagram's API changes, you update one internal service — not 50 places in your codebase.

Rule 3: Every activity must have a fallback to manual verification. If auto-verify fails, the system automatically routes to manual review queue. Customer sees "under manual review" — not a broken error. The experience degrades gracefully.

Rule 4: Monitor external dependencies with alerts. If Instagram API success rate drops below 90% in a 1-hour window, alert fires before restaurants even notice.
Q
Does every restaurant need the same QR code? What if they have multiple tables or locations?
Setup ▾
The answer
QR codes are restaurant-specific but not table-specific in MVP1. One QR per restaurant (outlet). This is the simplest, most reliable setup. The QR encodes a URL like platform.com/r/[restaurant-slug] — the same URL regardless of which table it's on. All tables get the same sticker/tent card.
Multi-location handling
Same brand, multiple outlets (e.g. Cafe X Koramangala + Cafe X Indiranagar):
Each outlet gets its own QR and its own customer database. Customers who visit both are tracked as separate visit histories per outlet. In Phase 2: cross-outlet visit pooling (visits at any outlet count toward the same loop) — requires a shared customer identity layer.

Table-level QR (future feature): Each table has a unique QR that encodes table number. Useful for: tracking which tables drive the most loyalty sign-ups, identifying if the QR placement at table 7 is causing drop-off. Not needed in MVP1 — pure analytics feature for later.

Dynamic vs static QR: Use dynamic QR codes — the printed code points to a redirect URL you control. If you ever need to change the destination (e.g., during a rebrand), you update the redirect without reprinting 50 table cards.

The biggest strategic question — answered completely

Q
Should your platform stay completely separate from restaurant POS, or deeply integrate?
Most important question ▾
The full answer — not simple
This is the question that defines your product strategy for the next 3 years. There is no universally right answer. The right answer depends on when you're asking it. Here's the complete thinking:
Fully standalone
QR check-in only. No POS connection. Visits logged by customer scan. Rewards verified manually.
✓ Ship in weeks not months
✓ Works with any POS
✓ No API dependency
✓ Easy to onboard any restaurant
✗ Can't auto-verify purchases
✗ Gameable (low % but real)
✗ No spend data
✗ Feels lightweight to tech-savvy owners
POS-integrated
Loyalty fires on every bill. Visit + spend data flows automatically. Zero staff effort on redemption.
✓ No gaming possible
✓ Spend-based loops
✓ Richer analytics
✓ Staff effort near zero
✗ 3–6 months to build per POS
✗ Locks you to POS vendors
✗ POS API reliability varies
✗ Complex onboarding
Hybrid (recommended)
Standalone now. POS integration offered as an add-on for restaurants who want it later.
✓ Ship fast
✓ Doesn't exclude any restaurant
✓ Integration is an upsell
✓ Prove product first
✗ Two codepaths to maintain
✗ Some data gaps in standalone
White-label inside POS
Build your loyalty engine as a module that POS companies embed. Petpooja or UrbanPiper as distribution.
✓ Massive distribution
✓ POS handles integration
✗ Lose brand control
✗ Revenue share cuts margin
✗ Dependent on partner
✗ Not for now
The phased decision
MVP1 (now): Fully standalone. QR + manual. No POS. This is not a compromise — it's the right choice for this stage. Your value is the experience layer, not the data plumbing.

Phase 2 (month 5): Build Petpooja webhook integration as an optional add-on. Restaurants on Petpooja get automatic visit logging. Restaurants not on Petpooja keep using QR. Both work. Integration is a premium feature, not a requirement.

Phase 3 (month 12): Evaluate the white-label/partnership question based on how distribution is going. If you have 50+ restaurants independently, you have leverage. If growth is slow, partnerships become more attractive.

Research shows integrated programs see 15–25% redemption rates vs under 10% for standalone — but this gap disappears if your standalone UX is significantly better than the integrated competitor's.
Q
If a restaurant is on Petpooja, how do you position against Petpooja's built-in loyalty?
Sales critical ▾
The honest competitive picture
Petpooja's built-in loyalty is a points module. It's deeply integrated with their billing — points fire automatically on every invoice. It's genuinely convenient for a Petpooja restaurant. You need a clear answer for why yours is better, not just different.
The answer — 5 things Petpooja loyalty cannot do
1. Branded experience: Petpooja loyalty looks like Petpooja. No per-restaurant theming. Your platform looks like the restaurant's own brand.
2. Social activities: Petpooja rewards visits and spend only. No review rewards, no Instagram activity, no referral loops. Your engagement layer is completely absent in their product.
3. WhatsApp automation: Petpooja sends SMS. WhatsApp open rates are 4× higher. Your automated trigger system via WhatsApp is the entire retention engine.
4. Gamified journey evolution: Petpooja has no evolving loop concept — it's static points forever. Your multi-layer journey system creates compounding engagement.
5. Customer insights: Petpooja's CRM part is widely reviewed as weak. Your customer segmentation, churn detection, and win-back flows are purpose-built for loyalty — not bolted onto a billing system.

Pitch: "Petpooja handles your billing. We handle your customer relationships. They're different jobs."
Q
What if a restaurant switches POS while using your platform?
Edge case ▾
The answer
This is why staying standalone in MVP1 is actually protective. If your platform is QR-based and doesn't depend on any POS, a restaurant switching from Petpooja to Restroworks has zero impact on you. Your system keeps working. Their new POS is irrelevant.

The moment you build deep POS integration, a restaurant switching POS becomes your problem too. You have to rebuild the integration for their new system or they churn from you when they churn from their POS.
Design principle
Your platform's core visit tracking must always have a non-POS fallback path. Even after you build POS integration, QR check-in should remain available. This makes you POS-agnostic by design — a genuine competitive advantage when selling to restaurants who resent vendor lock-in.

Update, maintenance, and evolution problems

Q
How do you push platform updates without breaking the experience for active customers mid-session?
Engineering ▾
The problem
You push a backend update at 2pm on a Saturday. A customer is mid-way through a loyalty check-in. The API response format changed. Their dashboard breaks. They see an error. At a restaurant. During lunch. This is real — it's happened to Reelo (the restaurant name bug is exactly this kind of issue) and it destroys trust immediately.
How to handle this
Rule 1 — Deploy windows: Never deploy during service hours. Indian restaurant peak hours: 12–3pm and 7–11pm daily. Deploy only between 3–6am on weekdays. Automate this constraint in your CI/CD pipeline — block deployments outside these windows.

Rule 2 — API versioning from Day 1: Your API endpoints are versioned: /api/v1/checkin. When you ship a breaking change, it goes to /api/v2/checkin. The old version stays active for 60 days. Customer-facing PWA keeps working even if it's running old client code.

Rule 3 — Feature flags: New features ship behind flags that are off by default. You enable them per-restaurant (try it on Restaurant A first, wait 48 hours, then roll out to all). The restaurant that discovers your bug first is not your most important customer.

Rule 4 — Staged rollouts: New backend code goes to 10% of traffic first. Monitor error rates. If error rate stays below 0.5%, roll to 100%. Automated rollback if error rate spikes.

Rule 5 — Status page: A public status page (statuspage.io or similar) shows uptime. Restaurant admins can check it before blaming their own Wi-Fi. Transparency builds trust.
Q
How do you update a restaurant's branding without disrupting their active customers?
Ops ▾
The problem
A restaurant changes their logo, rebrand colors, or changes their reward structure. Their customers might be mid-journey. Active coupons with old branding look inconsistent. Reward rules changing mid-loop feel like a scam.
Answer
Branding changes (cosmetic — logo, colors, hero image): These are always safe to update immediately. The customer's journey data doesn't change — only how it looks. Build a preview mode so the restaurant can see how the change looks before publishing.

Reward structure changes (loop N changed from 5 visits to 7 visits): Customers already in-progress on the old structure must be grandfathered in. Technically: tag their active journey record with the config version it started under. New sign-ups get the new structure. Existing customers finish their current loop under the old rules. This is non-negotiable — changing the rules mid-game destroys trust. Starbucks changed their reward structure in 2026 and faced massive backlash despite being a trillion-dollar brand. Your restaurant customers are much less forgiving.

Menu item reward changes (the free item changes): 30-day grace period. Existing unredeemed coupons must be honoured for the old reward OR offered an equivalent. Admin panel shows count of outstanding unredeemed coupons before allowing changes.
Q
How do you handle WhatsApp template changes when Meta forces an update?
External dependency ▾
The problem
Meta periodically changes WhatsApp Business API policies — template formats, character limits, category rules. In 2023 they recategorised millions of templates. In 2024 they changed pricing tiers. Each time this happens, your automated messages potentially stop working until you resubmit and get re-approved. This can take days.
Answer
1. Always maintain 2 approved templates per message type. Primary and backup. If primary gets rejected or disabled, backup fires automatically without manual intervention.
2. Monitor template status via API daily. Alert fires the moment any template changes status from APPROVED to anything else. You get ahead of it before restaurants notice.
3. Build a template management UI in your admin panel so you can update and resubmit templates without a code deploy. Today it takes an engineer. Tomorrow it should take a support person.
4. Graceful degradation: If WhatsApp delivery fails, fall back to SMS for critical messages (reward unlocked, expiry warning). SMS is less good but better than silence.
Q
How do you scale the platform from 10 restaurants to 100 without rewriting everything?
Architecture ▾
The answer
The multi-tenant architecture decision made in MVP1 determines whether scaling to 100 restaurants is a configuration exercise or a rebuild. Three things must be right from Day 1:
Three non-negotiable architecture decisions
1. Tenant isolation at the database level. Every restaurant's customer data is segregated — either by separate schema (PostgreSQL: schema-per-tenant) or by tenant_id on every table with row-level security. A bug in Restaurant A's code path must be physically incapable of reading Restaurant B's customer data. This is not just good practice — it's what allows you to sell to restaurants who ask "is my customer data safe?"

2. Asynchronous WhatsApp sending. WhatsApp triggers must go into a queue (Redis or SQS) and be processed by a worker, not fired synchronously during the check-in API call. If WhatsApp API is slow (it sometimes is), your check-in response time must not be affected. At 10 restaurants this doesn't matter. At 100 restaurants firing simultaneous Friday evening check-ins, it crashes your system without this separation.

3. Config-driven, not code-driven, customisation. Every restaurant-specific setting (loop size, reward type, brand colors, active activities) lives in a database config table — not in code. Adding a new restaurant is an insert, not a deployment. This sounds obvious but many early startups hard-code the first few restaurant configs. Then scaling becomes a code refactor.

Data ownership, privacy, and portability questions

Q
Who owns the customer data — you or the restaurant?
Legal + commercial ▾
This question will come up in every sales conversation
Restaurant owners are increasingly savvy about this — partly because of the Zomato situation where aggregators own customer relationships. The moment a restaurant owner asks "if I leave your platform, do I keep my customer list?" your answer determines whether they sign.
The right answer — and why it wins you deals
Legal position: The restaurant owns their customer data. You are a data processor, not a data controller. Customers sign up for a loyalty program at [Restaurant Name] — not at your platform. The data was collected under the restaurant's brand.

Practical position: Any restaurant can export their full customer database at any time — in a standard CSV format — from the admin panel. No exit fees. No delays. This is a feature, not a concession. Put it in your sales pitch: "Unlike Zomato, you own your customer data with us. Export it anytime."

Your data rights: You can use aggregated, anonymised data (e.g. "restaurants on our platform see 43% higher return rates") for marketing and product improvement. You cannot sell individual restaurant customer data to third parties or use it to target those customers for other restaurants. This must be explicit in your contract.

India regulatory context: India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act (DPDPA) 2023 came into force. You are a "data fiduciary" when you collect customer phone numbers. You must: have clear consent language at sign-up, have a data deletion mechanism, not transfer personal data outside India without consent.

Build a one-click "export my data" button in admin from Day 1. It's a trust signal and a legal requirement.
Q
Can you use data from Restaurant A to benefit Restaurant B? Should you?
Ethics + strategy ▾
The temptation and the danger
As you grow, you'll have data on thousands of diners across multiple Bangalore restaurants. You'll know that customers who visit a brewpub on Friday nights also tend to brunch on Sundays. You could theoretically use this to help Restaurant B target Restaurant A's customers. This would be extremely valuable — and extremely dangerous.
The answer
Never cross-sell customer data between restaurants without explicit customer consent. The moment this becomes known (and it will), every restaurant on your platform will immediately wonder if their customer list is being shared with competitors. You lose all accounts simultaneously.

What you CAN do:
→ Platform-level benchmarks: "Restaurants in Koramangala see an average return rate of 48%. You're at 31%. Here's what the top performers do differently." Anonymised, aggregated, safe.
→ Cross-restaurant discovery (with consent): In Phase 3, offer a feature where customers can opt-in to discover other restaurants on the platform. They explicitly choose this. It's not covert targeting.
→ Platform-wide campaigns to opted-in customers: "Food lovers in Bangalore — here are 3 restaurants with loyalty programs this week." Only customers who opted into platform-wide comms receive this.

Hard rule: restaurant customer data is never shared between restaurants, ever, without customer consent
Q
What data do you actually need, and what is safe not to collect?
Privacy design ▾
Data minimalism — collect only what drives product value
Must collect: Phone number (identity + WhatsApp channel). Visit timestamps. Reward states. Activity submissions.

Collect gradually (not at sign-up): Name (via WhatsApp after 2nd visit), birthday (optional, for birthday reward), location (only if needed for multi-outlet routing).

Never need to collect: Email (WhatsApp is primary), address, payment details, dietary preferences (unless restaurant specifically needs this for personalisation — opt-in feature).

Data retention policy (publish this): Active customer data: retained while loyalty account is active. Inactive (no activity 2 years): anonymised. Restaurant offboarding: data exported to restaurant, deleted from platform within 90 days.

Minimum data collection is both a privacy principle and a product advantage — less friction at sign-up = higher conversion

Staff, adoption, and the human problems

Q
Restaurant staff turnover in India is 40–60% annually. How do you build a system that doesn't depend on trained staff?
Most underestimated problem ▾
Why this kills loyalty programs
You train 3 staff members in January. By April, 2 of them are gone. The new staff have no idea the loyalty program exists. Customers try to redeem rewards. Staff are confused. Customers give up. The restaurant owner doesn't notice until 3 months later when redemption rates have collapsed.
Design for zero training — literally
Principle: the system must work if a brand-new staff member has received exactly zero training about it.

How:
1. Counter mode URL on a laminated card at every counter: "Customer has a reward? Go to [URL] and enter their 6-digit code." One sentence. One URL. One action.
2. The customer's phone does the explanation — the reward coupon says what it is in plain language. Staff don't need to know the system. They just need to read what the customer shows them and confirm it.
3. Every redemption sends a confirmation to the restaurant owner's WhatsApp in real-time: "Reward redeemed: 1 free cold brew for [customer name] at [time]." Owner knows it happened even if staff don't log it correctly.
4. Monthly "how your loyalty program is doing" report to restaurant owner — they see the numbers and naturally reinforce with staff if redemptions drop.

Goal: staff are couriers, not operators. The system does the thinking.
Q
The restaurant owner signed up but their manager actively discourages staff from promoting the loyalty program. What do you do?
Adoption blocker ▾
Why this happens
Restaurant managers are judged on speed and throughput, not customer retention. A loyalty redemption at a busy counter takes an extra 20 seconds. That's 20 seconds × every redemption = a bottleneck the manager resents. They don't actively resist — they just don't promote it, don't train new staff on it, and quietly deprioritise it. The program withers without anyone explicitly killing it.
Answer
Short-term: Make QR placement passive — the QR is on the table card, the bill folder, the entrance. Customers discover it themselves without staff needing to say a word. The program doesn't rely on staff promotion to acquire customers.

Medium-term: Build a staff incentive into the platform (Phase 2). "Staff members who process the most redemptions this week get a recognition shoutout." Small gamification for the staff themselves. Manager's incentive shifts when redemptions = something that reflects well on them.

The owner conversation: During your monthly check-in with the restaurant owner, surface the data: "Your sign-up rate from table QR scans is lower than similar restaurants. It usually means QR cards aren't visible or staff are removing them. Can we check placement together?" You're not criticising — you're showing data. Owners respond to data.
Q
How do you handle the restaurant admin who never logs into the dashboard?
Engagement ▾
The problem
50% of restaurant owners will log in during onboarding, browse for a week, then never open the admin dashboard again. The loyalty program runs on autopilot — which is fine — but the activity verification queue fills up, campaigns never get sent, and the owner starts to think "I'm paying ₹6,000/month for something I never use."
Answer — push, don't pull
Don't make them come to the dashboard. Bring the dashboard to them.

Weekly WhatsApp digest: Every Monday, the restaurant owner gets a WhatsApp message: "Last week: 23 new members, 8 rewards redeemed, 4 activity submissions waiting for your review. [tap to review]." One message. Key numbers. One action.

Alert-driven engagement: Only send a WhatsApp when something needs attention: "You have 3 activity submissions pending — customers are waiting for their rewards." Urgency through customer impact, not platform nag.

Monthly ROI report: Auto-generated, sent via WhatsApp as a PDF or link. "This month your loyalty program brought back 41 customers who hadn't visited in 30+ days. Estimated revenue: ₹28,000." This is the number that makes them renew.

Design principle: the admin panel is for power users. The WhatsApp digest is for everyone.

The 10 operational decisions every founder must make before launch
Checklist ▾
How do you verify a visit is real?
MVP1: Honour-based QR scan. Accept 3% gaming rate. Solve technically in Phase 2.
Who handles WhatsApp API?
Use a BSP (Interakt/Wati). Not direct Meta API. Submit all 7 templates 2 weeks before launch.
One WhatsApp number or per-restaurant?
MVP1: shared platform number. Phase 2: per-restaurant numbers as premium add-on.
How does staff verify redemption?
Counter mode: 6-digit code, one URL, one button. Zero training required.
What if internet is down at counter?
Coupon codes cached on customer device. Offline verification queue on counter mode. Never block a redemption for connectivity.
Who owns customer data?
Restaurant owns it. You process it. Export available anytime. This goes in the contract and in the sales pitch.
POS integration — yes or no?
No in MVP1. Petpooja webhook in Phase 2. Full API in Phase 3. Prove product first.
When can you deploy updates?
3–6am weekdays only. API versioning from Day 1. Feature flags for all new features.
How do inactive admins stay engaged?
Weekly WhatsApp digest. Alert on pending verifications. Monthly ROI report. Never assume they'll log in.
How do you handle a restaurant closing?
30-day wind-down protocol. Customer notifications. Full data export. 90-day data retention for disputes.
